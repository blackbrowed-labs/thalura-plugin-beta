# Changelog

All notable changes to the Thalura plugin are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog 1.1.0](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning 2.0.0](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Thalura — your lesson-planning and assessment assistant for Hamburg:** the core of Thalura — describe what you need in your own words and Thalura routes you to the right task: plan a whole school year or a teaching unit (Unterrichtseinheit), detail a single lesson (Stunde), create materials (Arbeitsblätter, Folien, Handouts, Lesetexte) and assessments (Lernkontrollen) with a task paper (Aufgabe) and grading rubric (Erwartungshorizont), differentiate for special needs, generate image prompts, get a teaching-method recommendation, or reflect on a finished unit. Everything is anchored in the official Hamburg curriculum (Bildungsplan) and BSB regulations and can be checked against them (compliance check). Supports Gymnasium and Stadtteilschule across English, Philosophy, Psychology, and Religion, with your own school profile, school details, and remembered preferences, your school branding on generated slide decks, and your school-internal curriculum (Schulinternes Curriculum). Works both in Claude Cowork and on the command line.
- **Unit library (Bibliothek) — reuse your units across classes and years:** shelve a finished teaching unit (Unterrichtseinheit) into your personal library and later assign it to another class or a new school year — Thalura copies the validated documents, leaves drafts and student submissions (Abgaben) untouched in the class folder, renames the assigned files for the new class (the old class's abbreviation does not stick), registers the unit in the target year's plan, and reminds you to adapt the material to the new group until you confirm it fits. Shelving a revised unit whose title is already on the shelf offers to keep it as a **new version (neue Fassung)** under the same library unit: every version is kept, when assigning you pick one (the newest by default), and after a reflection that ends in "reuse" Thalura offers to fold your changes back into the library. Archived library versions can be restored at any time.
- **Share units with colleagues (Einheiten teilen):** export a shelved unit from your library (Bibliothek) as a single file you can send to a colleague — it contains the validated documents and — unless you explicitly choose to include your drafts (Entwürfe) — no drafts, and never student submissions (Abgaben) or your class names. A received unit file is imported straight onto your shelf: Thalura checks the file thoroughly, warns you if it was created for another federal state (Bundesland) or school type (Schulform), and then the unit is assigned to one of your classes like any shelved unit.
- **Hand a unit over as plain files (Nur die Dokumente):** besides the re-importable Thalura package, you can now export a unit's finished documents as ordinary files in one ZIP — openable by any colleague with no Thalura needed, and containing no Thalura package envelope and no AI-generation notice (Erstellungshinweis) in the documents. When you export, Thalura asks which form you want, and — if the unit still has drafts (Entwürfe) — whether to include the in-progress drafts (they stay marked as drafts).
- **Backup & restore (Sicherung & Wiederherstellung) — move your whole workspace to a new computer:** save all your irreplaceable work — year plans, classes, your library (Bibliothek), every generated document, and your in-progress drafts (Entwürfe) — into a single backup file, leaving student submissions (Abgaben) and regenerable data behind. On a brand-new computer you simply restore — no prior setup needed: Thalura builds the workspace directly from the backup and completes everything the onboarding would have provided (including your branded slide template, or an honest note when it cannot be generated here); on a computer that already has units, Thalura safely adds only the library rather than overwriting your work, and it asks you to update the plugin first if the backup came from a newer version. A backup made in one display language (Anzeigesprache) restores correctly into a workspace set up in another — your documents' links keep working.
- **Aufgabenfolien — a task deck for the classroom screen:** for each lesson Thalura can produce a projection-ready slide deck of the student tasks (Aufgabenfolien), meant to stay on screen while students work so it is always visible what to do right now — one slide per work phase, not one task per slide. Its content is proposed while you plan the lesson, and the file is generated after the lesson is validated. The deck is on by default; you can switch it off (and back on) under `/thalura:config behaviour`.
- **Ready-to-print PDF for your validated materials:** when you validate a student-facing material — a worksheet (Arbeitsblatt), handout (Handout), reading text (Lesetext), slide deck (Foliensatz), or exam paper (Aufgabe) — Thalura automatically creates a PDF of it next to the editable file, ready to print, hand out, or upload to a school platform. If you revise the material and validate it again, the PDF is updated to match. A new advanced setting (`pdf_on_validation`) lets you extend PDF creation to all documents (including teacher-facing ones) or turn it off entirely; it is not asked during setup.
- **Report a problem (Fehler melden):** when something looks wrong, Thalura can generate a diagnostic report (Fehlerbericht) — a single text file with the technical context the developers need. You review the file and send it yourself by e-mail; Thalura never sends anything on its own, and the report never includes any student data.
- **Class continuity across school years:** link a class to its previous year (E9 → E10) — on request, or via a quick offer when you create a new class that continues a prior-year group (Thalura can also carry over reusable details like class size and differentiation). When planning or reflecting, Thalura can then draw on the group's earlier experience: what worked, which methods landed, recurring strengths and gaps. Reading prior years is read-only and never includes grades.
- **School-year transition, recognised from the conversation:** Thalura infers which school year you are planning for from what you say, and on first contact with a new school year proposes fitting class continuations along your state's grade progression (e.g. your E9a becomes an E10a; a grade-10 class continues as S1 in the Oberstufe; a cohort that finished its Abitur is recognised as completed). Nothing is created until you confirm, and data from past school years is never touched.
- **Clickable regulation citations — jump straight to the source:** where an official online version of the cited document is available, the citation becomes a clickable link that opens the original at (or near) the cited page — so you can check the quote and read its context in one click, instead of looking the document up yourself. This works in your generated documents (the curriculum anchoring of a unit plan, a compliance check, an assessment) and, where the surface allows it, directly in the chat. When no online source is available for a document, the citation stays exactly as before, with the document name and page number.
- **An always-current school year overview (Schuljahresübersicht) as a document:** all classes of the current school year with their teaching units (Unterrichtseinheiten) — each linked to its unit plan (Einheitenplanung) — plus the competency coverage (Kompetenzabdeckung) at a glance, with an Abitur countdown for final-phase courses; the document refreshes itself whenever your year planning changes.
- **Automatic image generation (Bildgenerierung) when a compatible tool is connected:** When an image-generation tool (Bildgenerierungs-Werkzeug) is connected, approved images in materials are generated automatically; without one, the existing manual placeholder flow (Platzhalter) is unchanged.
- **The official regulations arrive already read (schon nachgelesen):** Thalura now brings the passages of the official documents with it, already worked through once — so for the subjects and documents it already covers, a request that rests on the curriculum standards (Bildungsplan) or a BSB regulation is usually answered straight away instead of after a first read. The passages are set up in your workspace during setup and are there from that first day: the speed is no longer something you have to earn over weeks of use. How much is already read differs by subject, school type (Schulform), and document — for some regulations practically every section is included, for others only a part — so a genuine first read still happens: for a document outside the covered set, for your own school-internal curriculum (Schulinternes Curriculum), and for pages whose meaning sits in a diagram or a table, which are deliberately left to be looked at properly rather than pre-read. Whatever is read fresh is remembered just as before, so what was not covered on day one fills in exactly where you teach. An update brings newly added passages into a workspace you have long been using as well, and never replaces a passage Thalura has already read for you.
- **Include drafts in a shared Thalura package (Entwürfe teilen):** the re-importable Thalura package (Thalura-Paket) can now carry your in-progress drafts (Entwürfe) when you export a unit for a colleague — on request only and off by default, so a finished hand-over still carries none while a shared work-in-progress can. Imported drafts land in your library (Bibliothek) still clearly marked as drafts and are used for nothing until taken over: when you assign the unit to a class, Thalura asks whether to take the drafts over too, and taken-over drafts stay marked as drafts until you have reviewed them.
- **Gender-inclusive language (Gendern) for German documents:** choose how your generated German documents gender — a Hamburg-safe neutral default is applied automatically during setup, and you can change it any time under `/thalura:config profile`, where a worked example of every option is shown. Two registers: student-facing documents (neutral form, Beidnennung, Schüler:innen or Schüler\*innen) and teacher-facing planning documents (abbreviations like SuS, or written out). The chosen form is applied consistently across each document; English documents are unaffected.
- **Special-needs catalog (Förderbedarfs-Katalog):** differentiation (Differenzierung) and the support sections of unit and lesson plans draw on a single, consistent set of stored measures (Maßnahmen) for the recognised priority areas of support (Förderschwerpunkte) — ADHS, LRS, HB, DaZ, ASS, KB, SB, AUD — with a clear distinction between legal accommodation (Nachteilsausgleich) and pedagogical support (Förderung).
- **Safe revisions — your manual edits are always preserved:** when you ask Thalura to revise a material you have edited yourself — for example after swapping in your own picture or typing something into the text — everything Thalura did not generate itself is treated as your edit and stays untouched: nothing of yours is removed, reverted, or "tidied up" unless you or one of your Word comments explicitly asks for it, and if one of your edits is in the way of a requested change, Thalura asks you instead of deciding on its own. Thalura edits the existing file in place and derives differentiated variants (Differenzierung) from it, keeping your changes and the school header/footer and styles intact — so your edits carry into the variant unchanged.
- **Verifiable citations with the printed page number:** when Thalura cites a curriculum standard (Bildungsplan) or a regulation in a unit plan, a compliance check, or an assessment, the citation names the document and the printed page number as it appears in the source — read directly from the official document — so you can look it up and confirm it in seconds. Where a quoted passage runs across a page break, the citation says so in the familiar form „S. 23 f." instead of naming only the page the quote starts on — so you find the whole passage, not just its beginning.
- **Edit your profile after setup (`/thalura:config profile`):** change your name, abbreviation, and email — and especially **which subjects you teach (add or remove a subject)** — without redoing setup. Adding a subject sets up its folders and starts loading its materials and regulations automatically; removing one keeps all your existing files.
- **Edit your school details after setup (`/thalura:config school`):** change your school name, your branding, your lesson slots (Stundenraster) — adding, editing, or removing single and double periods, including a double with a break — and your federal state (Bundesland) / school type (Schulform). Each change is validated and confirmed before anything is saved. Branding applies to your slide presentations only; your Word documents (worksheets, handouts, plans, assessments) stay in the neutral look.
- **Name your files your way (`/thalura:config naming`):** view and adjust the naming patterns Thalura uses for your documents, override a single pattern or reset it to the default, with each pattern checked against the allowed building blocks before it is saved.
- **Manage your remembered preferences (`/thalura:config preferences`):** review and remove your validated preferences (liked or rejected methods, social-form tendencies, structure and formatting choices). The internal compliance pre-check is toggled under `/thalura:config behaviour`.
- **Choose your document and conversation language (`/thalura:config profile`):** set the language of your materials per subject, and your conversation language, directly in your profile.
- **Repair your workspace scaffold (`/thalura:setup` on an existing workspace):** running setup on an already-initialized workspace offers „Grundgerüst prüfen und vervollständigen": Thalura checks that every folder and starter file a fresh onboarding would have created is in place and creates whatever is missing, without touching your profile, settings, or documents.
- **Clickable indexes in your material overview and plans:** in the material overview (Materialübersicht) each material carries a label you click to open the file directly — `[DOCX]` (or `[PPTX]` for slide decks) opens the editable file, and once the material is validated an extra `[PDF]` opens the print-ready PDF next to it. The overview also lists materials that are still drafts (Entwürfe), marked „(Entwurf)", and refreshes whenever your materials change. In the unit plan (Einheitenplanung) and the lesson plans (Verlaufsplan), the materials and lessons they mention become clickable links too, so you jump straight to the file instead of hunting through folders. The links are relative, so they keep working when you move your Thalura folder or restore a backup (Sicherung), and a link left dangling by a rename outside Thalura heals itself the next time that document is regenerated.
- **Your name is the author of every generated document:** each worksheet (Arbeitsblatt), plan, slide deck (Foliensatz), and assessment (Lernkontrolle) Thalura creates carries your name as its author in the document properties (File → Eigenschaften / File → Properties), together with the document's own title and the real creation date. A colleague you share a file with sees you as the author, not a tool.
- **Thalura tells you when it reads a regulation fresh — and which documents it is reading:** before reading an official regulation — e.g. the curriculum standards (Bildungsplan) — on a topic for the first time, Thalura says in the chat that it is looking the passage up in the original documents and **names each document it is reading**; for longer reads it also shows a short **progress list with one entry per document**, so you can see what is happening and how far it has got. When a task draws on several regulations, Thalura reads them together in manageable steps rather than strictly one after another, so the wait stays closer to the longest single document than to all of them added together. It notes that this first read can take a moment and that later questions on the same topic come back faster. Naming the documents is an announcement, not a question — nothing waits on your agreement; and it happens whether or not the passage turns out to be one Thalura already remembers, in which case the answer comes straight back with nothing read again.
- **An optional list of your standard supplies (Standardmaterial):** record which everyday classroom supplies (e.g. Post-its, crepe tape (Kreppband), pens) you actually have — lesson planning then gently prefers those items, without ever restricting proposals or complaining about missing material. Optional and sensibly pre-filled; editable anytime via `/thalura:config`.
- **Handbook page — reliability and speed (Verlässlichkeit und Tempo):** the guide explains why tasks grounded in the official regulations — planning a unit, auditing an assessment, a compliance check, or a plain curriculum (Bildungsplan) question — can take a moment before results appear (Thalura reads the original regulation itself, for accuracy), and why that wait shrinks with continued use.
- **Handbook page — beta testing and feedback (Beta testen und Rückmeldung geben):** a guide for beta testers: what to look at after each working step — from the year plan to the grading rubric (Erwartungshorizont) — how to capture what you noticed (what you asked, subject and grade, expected vs. observed), how to send feedback (Rückmeldung), and what to keep in mind before sharing files from your Thalura folder.
- **A README in the data folder:** Thalura's internal `data` folder contains a short README explaining that Thalura manages this folder, what happens if its contents are changed, and the two places where you add files yourself (your school logo, and your school-internal curricula (Schulinternes Curriculum) as PDFs). It is created during setup, appears in existing workspaces with the next plugin update, and is never overwritten once present.

### Changed
- **Setting up in Claude Cowork now carries one required step: Thalura has to run on your own computer.** If a task runs in the cloud instead, none of your citations are clickable, the year overview (Schuljahresübersicht) is not written, your documents are produced without your school's template — no header and footer, no author name — and every regulation is read from scratch again. None of it is reported as an error, so the result looks finished. The handbook and both READMEs now say so up front, name the setting to switch off (**Settings → Cowork** → "Run new tasks in the cloud", then start a new task), and give one check you can do in seconds: after your first teaching unit (Unterrichtseinheit) is registered, the year overview has to be sitting in your teacher folder. Worth checking on documents you have already produced. This is where things stand today; a change will be noted here.
- **German lead-in for the /-menu entries:** most Thalura entries in the /-composer picker now open with a short German label (Beschriftung) before the technical routing text, so the menu is readable at a glance in your language. (Two entries — workspace backup (Sicherung) and the unit library (Bibliothek) — keep their English text for now.)

### Fixed
- **Regulation passages that were being skipped are now read:** in a number of places the internal page index ended a section one page too early, so a paragraph that continued onto the next page — for example the passage on writing support (Schreibrahmen) in the Rahmenvorgaben Sprachbildung — was never read and could never be cited. This has now been checked systematically across the whole set of official documents (Gymnasium and Stadtteilschule, all subjects): every affected page range is corrected, so those passages — in the Abiturrichtlinie, the APO-AH, the curriculum standards (Bildungsplan), the Sprachbildung and Aufgabengebiete guidance, and more — are now available to Thalura and to the citations in your documents.
- **Reading the regulations no longer runs away with time:** a passage Thalura has already looked up is now reused without opening the document again — a remembered passage normally costs no read at all, instead of quietly being read a second time. The documents are read side by side, so the wait follows the longest single document rather than the sum of them all. Requests that touch the official regulations — planning a unit, auditing an assessment, a compliance check, or a plain curriculum (Bildungsplan) question — therefore come back far faster and far more predictably.
- **Stored quotations from official regulations are kept word for word:** when Thalura quotes a passage from the curriculum standards (Bildungsplan) or another official regulation and remembers it for later reuse, the stored quotation could in rare cases lose a word on the way into that memory — and the shortened version was then reused in later documents without anyone noticing. Passages are now stored exactly as they appear in the original. A passage already stored in an earlier session is not repaired by this; it is replaced the next time that part of the document is read afresh.
- **A remembered passage can no longer come from the wrong part of the document:** when Thalura reached for a passage it had already looked up, it could in rare cases hand back a passage from a **different part of the same document** — one whose heading merely resembled the heading your question was about — and nothing in the answer gave that away. A passage is now filed and found only under the heading it was actually read at; where Thalura cannot place that heading with certainty, it reads the original afresh instead of reaching for the closest match. An uncertain lookup therefore costs you a moment's reading rather than a confidently wrong quotation. What was already filed under the wrong heading in an earlier session is not repaired by this and can still be handed back: because a remembered passage is returned without opening the document, such an entry is replaced only when that part of the document is read afresh — after the regulation is republished, or when a fresh read is asked for.
- **A passage Thalura could not confirm is no longer stuck that way:** when a remembered passage turns out not to answer your question — because the decisive lines sat on pages the first read did not cover — Thalura now opens the original again for it, instead of coming back with the same incomplete answer to every later question. It is the one case in which a remembered passage is read a second time, and what Thalura reads afresh takes the place of what it had remembered — so later questions draw on the completed passage too.
- **Regulations are remembered again on computers set to certain language settings:** on a computer whose system language setting is not Unicode-based, everything Thalura had already looked up in an official regulation was quietly forgotten — a passage was re-read from the original document on every single request, and a finished read was never stored for next time. Because German text (Umlaute, §, Gedankenstriche) is the normal case in these documents, this affected effectively every regulation Thalura reads. Nothing looked wrong: there was no error message, no missing output, only the wait coming back every time. Storing a finished read, and the lookup helper that reads it back, now work independently of the computer's language settings, so what Thalura looks up is kept instead of being discarded. The remaining half is now closed as well: a saved passage is handed back on later questions, the safeguards deciding *which* passage may be handed back work again, and the instruction that sends a reader to the original document is no longer lost on the way. On such a computer these steps had simply not happened — again with nothing to see: no error, no missing output. One further point of correctness is fixed with them: where a request contained a character the computer could not read, Thalura could settle on the wrong part of a document and hand that passage back; it now reads the original afresh instead.
- **Page references now match the language of what you are reading:** a citation in a German document or a German chat reply names the page as "S. 27", an English one as "p. 27" — previously a compliance check could show the English "p." in otherwise German output. Where a citation covers several pages, the span is written the same way everywhere ("S. 23–25").
