# Short Test (Kurztest)

A brief, low-stakes written assessment used to check comprehension, vocabulary, or key concepts within a single lesson or short sequence. The least formal written assessment format.

## Structure and Sections

A short test (Kurztest) is a compact written assessment that does not count as a formal Klassenarbeit (Klassenarbeit) or Klausur (Klausur). It falls under "laufende Unterrichtsarbeit" (ongoing classroom work) and serves as a Lernerfolgskontrolle (learning progress check).

Typical structure:
1. **Single task block** — 3–8 items testing a focused skill or knowledge area
2. **No formal choice** — all items are mandatory (unlike Klausuren which may offer task choice)
3. **Closed or semi-open formats** — gap-fill, matching, short-answer, or brief constructed response

Short tests do not require formal operators (Operatoren) in grades 5–8. From grade 9 onward, operators may be used per BP Sek I Teil C, Section 3a, p. 7.

## Operator Requirements per Performance Level (Anforderungsbereich)

Short tests primarily target AB I (reproduction) and AB II (reorganization/transfer). AB III (evaluation/reflection) is optional and typically limited to one item.

| AB Level | Typical Weight | Example Operators (English) |
|----------|---------------|---------------------------|
| AB I | 40–60% | name, describe, reproduce, list |
| AB II | 30–50% | explain, compare, categorize, summarize |
| AB III | 0–20% | evaluate, justify, assess (optional) |

No formal AB distribution is mandated by regulation for short tests. The distribution above reflects pedagogical best practice per BP Sek I Teil C, Section 3a, p. 7 (Reproduktion to Problemlösung) and BP Sek II Teil C, Section 3a, p. 7 (all Verständnisebenen).

## Number and Type of Tasks

- Typically 3–8 items in a single block
- No formal minimum or maximum task count regulated
- Tasks should be answerable within the allotted time without time pressure
- A mix of item types (e.g., 4 vocabulary items + 2 short-answer comprehension items) is common

## Permitted Aids (Hilfsmittel)

- Generally no aids permitted (closed-book)
- Teacher may permit specific aids at their discretion (e.g., operator list from grade 9 onward)
- For English: monolingual dictionary may be permitted at teacher's discretion

## Subject-Specific Notes

### English

- Vocabulary tests (Vokabeltests) are the most common short test type in English; these use a separate `vocabulary_test` format ID
- Short tests in English may assess reading comprehension, grammar, or listening micro-skills
- Language assessment criteria: communicative success and expression weighted higher than correctness (BP Sek I Teil C, Section 7g, p. 22; BP Sek II Teil C, Section 8h, p. 24)
- All tasks and responses are in English (target language)

### Philosophy

- Short tests may check knowledge of philosophical concepts, thinkers, or argument structures
- Assessment criteria include Wahrnehmungs-/Deutungskompetenz and Argumentations-/Urteilskompetenz
- Tasks should require more than mere reproduction — even brief answers should demonstrate understanding of philosophical concepts

### Psychology

- Psychology at Gymnasium is typically only available as an elective in Jg. 10; short tests check knowledge of psychological concepts, paradigms, or research methods
- Assessment criteria include Fachkompetenz, Methodenkompetenz, Urteilskompetenz, and Handlungskompetenz
- Tasks use materialgebundene Aufgaben (material-based tasks) with fachbezogene Texte, Fallbeispiele, or Untersuchungsergebnisse where appropriate
- Content organized by Lernjahre and modules (M1–M9); tasks should reference relevant psychologische Hauptstroemungen (paradigms)
- Written in German

### Religion

- Short tests may check knowledge of theological concepts, biblical references, or ethical frameworks
- Assessment criteria include Sachkompetenz, Deutungskompetenz, and Urteilskompetenz
- Tasks should connect factual knowledge to interpretive and evaluative competencies where possible
