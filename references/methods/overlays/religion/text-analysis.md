# Religion — Text-Based Methods

## Close Reading (Textanalyse)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Reading sacred texts (heilige Texte), theological sources (theologische Quellen), and ethical treatises is foundational to Religion instruction. Close reading of biblical passages, papal encyclicals, Reformation documents, or contemporary theological essays develops subject competency (Sachkompetenz) and interpretive competency (Deutungskompetenz).

---

## Reading Log (Lesetagebuch)

**Rating:** moderate

**Notes:** Tracking engagement with religious texts over time — personal responses to scripture readings, theological reflections, and evolving interpretations. Supports the Bildungsplan's emphasis on biographical learning (biografisches Lernen) and Selbstreflexion. Most effective in extended reading phases.

---

## Literature Circles

**Rating:** moderate

**Notes:** Useful for comparative religious text study (vergleichende Textarbeit) — small groups each read a different sacred text, theological perspective, or denominational position on the same theme, then share findings. Develops dialogue competency (Dialogkompetenz) through structured peer exchange.

---

## Argument Reconstruction (Argumentrekonstruktion)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Theological argumentation (theologische Argumentation) and ethical reasoning (ethische Argumentation) require precise reconstruction of arguments — e.g., analyzing Thomas Aquinas' proofs for God's existence, Kant's moral argument, or contemporary bioethics positions. Core method for developing judgment competency (Urteilskompetenz).

---

## Hermeneutical Circle (Hermeneutischer Zirkel)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** THE core methodology for Religion — hermeneutical reading of sacred texts (hermeneutische Texterschließung) is the fundamental approach to scripture and theological sources. Moving between part and whole, between text and context, between historical meaning and present-day significance. Central to every Bildungsplan strand from Sek I through Abitur.
