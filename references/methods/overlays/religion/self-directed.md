# Religion — Self-Directed & Station-Based Learning

## Station Learning (Stationenlernen / Lerntheke)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Highly effective for world religion stations (Weltreligionen-Stationen) — students rotate through stations on Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Buddhism, Hinduism with varied materials (texts, images, artifacts, audio). Also strong for liturgical year (Kirchenjahr) topics and ethical theme rotations. Supports subject competency (Sachkompetenz) through multi-sensory engagement.

---

## WebQuest

**Rating:** moderate

**Notes:** Research on religious communities (Religionsgemeinschaften), interreligious topics, or contemporary faith-in-society questions. Useful for self-directed exploration but requires well-curated source materials to ensure academic quality and sensitivity. Best suited for Sek II project phases.

---

## Project-Based Learning (Projektarbeit)

**Rating:** moderate

**Notes:** Interfaith projects (interreligiöse Projekte), community engagement documentation, or comparative religion research are possible but not standard methodology. Works best for extended Sek II units where students investigate a theological or ethical question in depth over multiple sessions.

---

## Task-Based Learning / TBL

**Rating:** none

**Notes:** English-specific methodology designed around communicative language tasks. No direct application in Religion instruction, where the focus is on theological content and ethical reasoning (ethische Urteilsbildung) rather than language proficiency.
