# Religion — Creative & Performative Methods

## Standbild / Freeze Frame (Tableau Vivant)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Powerful for embodying biblical scenes, ethical dilemmas (ethische Dilemmata), and interpersonal dynamics in religious narratives. Supports perspective-taking (Perspektivenwechsel) — a central competency in Hamburg Religion curriculum. Students physically represent power relations, moral conflicts, or key moments in sacred stories.

---

## Role Play & Simulation (Rollenspiel)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Biblical re-enactment (biblische Nacherzählung), ethical scenario exploration, and interreligious dialogue (interreligiöser Dialog) simulations. Central to Religion teaching for making abstract theological concepts experiential. Debriefing phase is essential to connect the experience to theological reflection.

---

## Hot Seat (Heißer Stuhl)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Inhabiting biblical or historical religious figures — e.g., a student as Moses, Luther, or a modern-day faith leader. Audience questions deepen understanding of theological positions and historical context. Particularly effective for developing interpretive competency (Deutungskompetenz) and empathy with perspectives distant from students' own.

---

## Creative Writing

**Rating:** moderate

**Notes:** Faith narratives (Glaubenserzählungen), prayer texts, meditation reflections, or letters from biblical characters. Supports creative theological reflection (theologische Reflexion) but is not the primary methodology in Religion curriculum. Most effective when connected to specific textual or ethical content.

---

## Digital Storytelling

**Rating:** low

**Notes:** Presenting religious biographies, community portraits, or social justice narratives. Time-intensive relative to Religion-specific learning outcomes. Occasional use for project work (Projektarbeit) on interreligious topics or documenting community engagement.
