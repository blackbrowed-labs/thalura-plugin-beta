# Religion — Cooperative Learning Methods

## Think-Pair-Share

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Strong for personal reflection (Selbstreflexion) before sharing on faith and ethics topics. The individual thinking phase is particularly valuable for sensitive religious questions where students need time to formulate their position before dialogue.

---

## Placemat (Placemat-Methode)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Effective for collecting diverse perspectives on ethical and theological questions (ethisch-theologische Fragestellungen) — e.g., different positions on euthanasia, perspectives from different religious traditions on a shared moral question. The written format ensures all voices are heard.

---

## Kugellager (Inside-Outside Circle / Ball Bearing)

**Rating:** moderate

**Notes:** Useful for interreligious dialogue (interreligiöser Dialog) practice — students rotate through partners, explaining core beliefs, practices, or ethical positions from different religious traditions. Requires careful framing to maintain respectful exchange.

---

## Jigsaw (Gruppenpuzzle / Expertengruppen)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Ideal for world religion expert groups (Expertengruppen) — each group studies one tradition (Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Buddhism, Hinduism), then teaches others. Also effective for distributing different theological perspectives or biblical text passages across groups.

---

## Gallery Walk (Museumsgang / Galerierundgang)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Presenting theological position papers (theologische Positionspapiere), interreligious comparisons, or visual representations of religious concepts. Students engage with multiple perspectives at their own pace, supporting interpretive competency (Deutungskompetenz).

---

## Snowballing (Schneeball-Methode)

**Rating:** moderate

**Notes:** Building consensus on ethical questions (ethische Fragestellungen) through progressive group merging. Works well when seeking shared ground across different faith perspectives, though the consensus-building goal can be in tension with preserving legitimate religious diversity.
