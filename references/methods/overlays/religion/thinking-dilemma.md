# Religion — Thinking & Dilemma Methods

## Thought Experiments (Gedankenexperimente)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Theological-ethical thought experiments (theologisch-ethische Gedankenexperimente) are central to Religion instruction — e.g., theodicy scenarios ("If God is good and omnipotent, why does suffering exist?"), trolley-type dilemmas with a theological dimension, or hypothetical encounters between representatives of different faith traditions. Develops judgment competency (Urteilskompetenz).

---

## Dilemma Discussion (Dilemma-Diskussion)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Ethical reasoning (ethische Urteilsbildung) is core to Religion — moral dilemmas at the intersection of faith and contemporary life: end-of-life decisions, social justice vs. personal faith, religious freedom vs. secular law. Kohlberg's moral development stages serve as both content and analytical framework, connecting to the Bildungsplan's ethics strand.

---

## Opinion Line (Positionslinie / Meinungslinie)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Positioning on faith and ethics questions — making beliefs visible and discussable. Students physically position themselves on a spectrum (e.g., "God intervenes in the world" vs. "God does not intervene"), then articulate their reasoning. Supports perspective-taking (Perspektivenwechsel) and reveals the diversity of positions within a learning group.

---

## Conceptual Analysis (Begriffsanalyse)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Analyzing core theological concepts (theologische Grundbegriffe) — Gott, Glaube, Sünde, Gerechtigkeit, Erlösung, Hoffnung. Testing definitions against scriptural usage, theological tradition, and students' lived experience. Particularly valuable for clarifying terms that carry different meanings across religious traditions, supporting interreligious dialogue (interreligiöser Dialog).
