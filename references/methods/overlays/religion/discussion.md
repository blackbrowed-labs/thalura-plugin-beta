# Religion — Discussion & Dialogue Methods

## Socratic Dialogue (Sokratisches Gespräch)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Central to theological-ethical discourse (theologisch-ethischer Diskurs). The structured questioning approach mirrors the tradition of theological inquiry — examining assumptions about God, faith, suffering, and justice. Develops judgment competency (Urteilskompetenz) through disciplined reasoning rather than assertion.

---

## Fishbowl Discussion (Fishbowl-Diskussion)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Excellent for interreligious dialogue (interreligiöser Dialog) and ethical controversies — inner circle represents different theological or ethical positions while outer circle observes argumentation quality. Makes the structure of theological reasoning visible and supports dialogue competency (Dialogkompetenz).

---

## Pro-Contra Debate (Pro-Kontra-Debatte / Podiumsdiskussion)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Theological and ethical argumentation (theologisch-ethische Argumentation) is core to Religion instruction — e.g., debates on euthanasia, religious freedom, social justice, theodicy. Develops judgment competency (Urteilskompetenz) and the ability to argue from positions that may differ from one's own.

---

## Philosophical Conversation (Philosophisches Gespräch)

**Rating:** high

**Notes:** Religion is explicitly a dialogical and reflective discipline. The open-ended, inquiry-driven format is ideal for theological questions (theologische Fragestellungen) — "What is God?", "What makes an action morally good?", "How do we respond to suffering?" Significant overlap with Philosophy; Religion adds the dimension of faith traditions and lived spiritual experience.
