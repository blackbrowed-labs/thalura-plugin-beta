# Self-Directed & Station-Based Learning

Methods that give students autonomy over pace, path, or product. Good for differentiation and longer project phases. Require more preparation but build independence.

---

## Station Learning (Stationenlernen / Lerntheke)

**Grades:** 5–10 (less common in Oberstufe — students work more independently at that level)
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Übung
**Time:** 45–90 min (full lesson or double period)

**Procedure:**
1. Set up 4–8 stations around the room, each with a different task or aspect of the topic. Stations should be self-contained with clear instructions.
2. Mix station types: reading tasks, listening tasks, writing tasks, creative tasks, digital tasks, practice exercises.
3. Provide a **station plan** (Laufzettel): students track which stations they've completed.
4. Include **mandatory stations** (core content) and **optional stations** (extension/enrichment).
5. Students work at their own pace, individually or in pairs. Teacher circulates and supports.
6. Plenary wrap-up: synthesize key findings from across stations.

**Differentiation built in:**
- Difficulty levels marked with stars (★, ★★, ★★★).
- Help cards available at each station.
- Extension tasks for fast finishers.

**Avoid when:**
- Stations are just worksheets at different tables (genuine variety in task types is essential)
- No quality control: students rush through to finish first rather than engaging deeply
- Preparation time isn't available (well-designed stations take significant prep)
- Topic doesn't lend itself to modular division (some content needs sequential teaching)

## WebQuest

**Grades:** 7–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung
**Time:** 2–4 lessons

**Procedure:**
1. Teacher creates a structured online research task with:
   - **Introduction:** context and a motivating scenario/question.
   - **Task:** what students must produce (report, presentation, website, poster).
   - **Process:** step-by-step instructions for the research.
   - **Resources:** curated list of pre-selected, age-appropriate websites.
   - **Evaluation:** criteria for assessing the product.
   - **Conclusion:** reflection prompt.
2. Students work in groups, following the process and using the curated resources.
3. Product creation and presentation.

**English examples:** Research a country or culture, investigate a historical event related to a novel, compare media coverage of an issue.
**Philosophy examples:** Research a philosophical school of thought, investigate an ethical controversy from multiple perspectives.

**Avoid when:**
- Students are simply told "Google it" (that's not a WebQuest — curated resources and structured process are essential)
- Internet access is unreliable
- Resources haven't been vetted for quality and age-appropriateness
- The topic can be answered by a single search (WebQuests are for complex, multi-source tasks)

## Project-Based Learning (Projektarbeit)

**Grades:** 7–S4
**Phase:** Transfer (extended, often end-of-unit)
**Time:** 1–3 weeks (with regular check-ins)

**Procedure:**
1. Define a driving question or real-world challenge (e.g. "How should our city handle homelessness?", "Create a literary magazine", "Design an ethical code for AI").
2. Students form project groups. Each group plans their approach and divides responsibilities.
3. Research, creation, and iteration phases with teacher check-ins (milestones).
4. Product: something authentic — a presentation, podcast, video, website, exhibition, event, written report.
5. Public presentation: share with the class, other classes, parents, or the school community.
6. Individual and group reflection: What did I learn? What would I do differently?

**Key principles:**
- Student voice and choice in how they approach the project.
- Authenticity: the product serves a real purpose or audience.
- Sustained inquiry: not a one-day activity.
- Reflection and revision built into the process.

**Avoid when:**
- No time for the full project cycle (rushed projects produce shallow results)
- No clear learning objectives behind the project (it should practice specific skills, not just be "fun")
- Assessment focuses only on the product, not the process (include process documentation)
- Students lack the self-management skills for extended independent work (build up gradually from smaller tasks)

## Task-Based Learning / TBL

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Übung
**Time:** 30–60 min per task cycle

**Procedure:**
1. **Pre-task:** Teacher introduces the topic and the task. Provides vocabulary, model language, or a demonstration. Activates prior knowledge.
2. **Task:** Students perform a communicative task in pairs or groups using English. The focus is on **meaning** (completing the task), not **form** (grammatical accuracy). Examples:
   - Plan a trip using a budget
   - Rank solutions to a problem and justify your ranking
   - Compare two texts and present differences
   - Solve a mystery using clue cards
3. **Planning:** Students prepare to report their results to the class.
4. **Report:** Groups present their task outcomes. Class compares approaches.
5. **Language focus:** Teacher addresses specific language points that arose during the task (errors, useful phrases, new structures). This is where grammar instruction happens — in context, after the communicative need has been felt.

**Avoid when:**
- The "task" is actually a grammar exercise in disguise (genuine tasks have a non-linguistic outcome)
- No language focus phase follows (students repeat errors without correction)
- Students lack the minimum English to attempt the task (provide enough scaffolding)
- Philosophy classes (TBL is an English language teaching methodology)
