# Media & Analysis Methods

Methods for working with non-text media (film, images, cartoons) and the Hamburg-specific skill of Sprachmittlung. Primarily English-focused.

---

## Film / Video Analysis (Filmanalyse)

**Grades:** 7–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung
**Time:** Variable (clip analysis: 20–30 min; full film: multiple lessons)

**Procedure:**
1. **Pre-viewing:** Activate prior knowledge. Set a viewing task (focus questions, observation sheet).
2. **While viewing:** Students take structured notes. Pause at key moments for prediction or reaction.
3. **Post-viewing analysis:** Discuss content, then analyze cinematic techniques:
   - Camera angles and movements (close-up, long shot, tracking)
   - Editing (cuts, pace, juxtaposition)
   - Sound (dialogue, music, silence, sound effects)
   - Mise-en-scène (lighting, color, costume, setting)
4. Connect cinematic choices to **meaning**: How does the technique create a specific effect or convey a message?
5. Written or spoken response: film review, comparative analysis, creative response.

**Variations:**
- Sound-off viewing: watch without sound first, predict dialogue/music.
- Screenshot analysis: freeze a frame and analyze it as a still image.
- Film vs. text: compare a scene to the book version.

**Avoid when:**
- Film is shown as entertainment without analytical framework
- No pre-/post-viewing tasks (passive watching ≠ learning)
- Film is shown over multiple lessons without sustained engagement tasks between viewings
- The film is not age-appropriate or exceeds FSK rating for the grade

## Image / Cartoon Analysis (Bild- / Karikaturanalyse)

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Einstieg, Erarbeitung
**Time:** 15–25 min

**Procedure:**
1. **Description phase:** What do you see? List all visible elements without interpretation.
2. **Analysis phase:** How is the image constructed? (composition, color, perspective, symbols, exaggeration in cartoons, text elements)
3. **Interpretation phase:** What is the message? What is the creator's intention? What values or assumptions are embedded?
4. **Evaluation phase:** Do you agree with the message? Is it effective? What alternative perspectives exist?

**For cartoons specifically:** Identify exaggeration, labeling, symbolism, irony, and the target of criticism.

**Variations:**
- Silent image: students write their interpretation before any discussion (avoids groupthink).
- Image comparison: two images on the same topic from different perspectives.
- Student-created cartoon: after analysis, students create their own cartoon on a related issue.

**Avoid when:**
- Image is shown without structured analysis steps (students just say "I like it" or "I don't get it")
- Cultural context needed to understand the image hasn't been provided
- Using as a time-filler without connecting to the unit's learning goals

## Sprachmittlung / Mediation

**Grades:** 7–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Transfer
**Time:** 20–35 min

**Procedure:**
1. Set a **communicative scenario**: a German-speaking person needs information conveyed to an English-speaking person (or vice versa). The scenario defines the audience, purpose, and register.
2. Provide a German source text (article, brochure, dialogue, data, instructions).
3. Students transfer the **relevant content** into English — this is NOT translation. They:
   - Select what's relevant for the target audience
   - Summarize and restructure
   - Adapt register and cultural references
   - Add explanations where needed for the English-speaking audience
4. Compare student mediations: What did you include/omit and why?
5. Discuss the difference between translation and mediation.

**Variations:**
- Reverse mediation: English source → German target (for advanced classes).
- Oral mediation: student acts as interpreter in a simulated conversation.
- Cultural mediation: explain a culturally specific concept (e.g. Gymnasium, Abitur) to an English speaker.

**Avoid when:**
- Students lack the English proficiency to express the ideas (scaffold with vocabulary support)
- Source text is too long (excerpt the relevant section)
- Task is framed as translation (emphasize communicative purpose and audience awareness)
- Philosophy classes (this is an English-only methodology)
