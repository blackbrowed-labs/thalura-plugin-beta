# Writing & Reflection Methods

Methods for structuring written thought — essential for essay skills in both subjects, exam preparation, and metacognitive development.

---

## Process Writing / Scaffolded Essay Writing

**Grades:** 5–S4 (scaffolding intensity decreases with age)
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Sicherung, Transfer
**Time:** Spans multiple lessons for the full cycle

**Procedure:**
1. **Pre-writing:** Brainstorm, mind map, or outline. Define thesis/central question.
2. **Drafting:** Write a first draft. Focus on ideas and structure, not perfection.
3. **Peer feedback:** Partners read drafts using a guided feedback sheet (e.g. "Is the thesis clear?", "Which argument is strongest?", "Where do you need more evidence?").
4. **Revision:** Students revise based on feedback. Focus on argument, structure, clarity.
5. **Editing:** Proofread for language, grammar, spelling.
6. **Final version:** Submit or present.

**Scaffolding by grade:**
- Grades 5–6: sentence starters, paragraph frames, model texts. Focus on paragraph structure.
- Grades 7–8: thesis statements, topic sentences, evidence integration. Introduction–body–conclusion.
- Grades 9–10: complex argumentation, counterarguments, nuanced conclusions.
- Oberstufe: independent essay planning, source integration, academic register.

**Avoid when:**
- Assessment is timed/exam conditions (process writing is for learning, not testing)
- Every essay goes through the full cycle (sometimes a quick draft is fine — save the full process for important pieces)
- Peer feedback quality is low (teach students how to give useful feedback first)

## Concept Mapping / Mind Mapping

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Einstieg, Erarbeitung, Sicherung
**Time:** 10–20 min

**Procedure:**
1. Central concept/topic in the middle of the page.
2. Students branch outward with related ideas, sub-concepts, examples, connections.
3. **Key rule for concept maps:** connections must be labeled (arrows with relationship words like "causes", "is an example of", "contradicts"). This distinguishes concept maps from simple mind maps.
4. Compare maps in pairs. Identify gaps, disagreements, new connections.
5. Use as a planning tool for essays or discussions.

**Variations:**
- Collaborative concept map: groups build a shared map on a large poster.
- Digital mapping: tools like MindMeister, Coggle, or Miro.
- Reverse mapping: start with a completed map and deconstruct it into prose.

**Avoid when:**
- Used as decoration rather than genuine thinking tool (maps should reveal relationships, not just list terms)
- Students produce only surface associations without deeper connections
- Overused as a default activity for every topic (varies diminish over time)

## Philosophical Essay (Erörterung / Philosophischer Essay)

**Grades:** 9–S4
**Phase:** Transfer, Leistungsüberprüfung
**Time:** 45–90 min (exam format) or multi-lesson (process)

**Procedure:**
1. Thesis or question is provided (or student formulates one based on a text/topic).
2. **Structure:**
   - Introduction: clarify the question, define key terms, state your approach.
   - Main body: present arguments and counterarguments. Engage with philosophical positions. Use examples and thought experiments.
   - Conclusion: weigh the arguments, state your reasoned position, acknowledge remaining tensions.
3. **Key quality criteria:**
   - Philosophical depth (not just opinions — engagement with concepts, theories, and logical reasoning)
   - Structural clarity (visible argument progression)
   - Precise use of philosophical terminology
4. Peer review against criteria before final submission.

**Variations:**
- Dialectical essay: thesis → antithesis → synthesis (Hegelian structure).
- Text-based essay (Texterörterung): respond to and critically engage with a provided philosophical text.
- English argumentative essay: similar structure but with emphasis on rhetorical effectiveness and evidence.

**Avoid when:**
- Students haven't practiced argument structure in simpler formats first (build up from paragraph-level arguments)
- The topic is too narrow for genuine argumentation (a good philosophical essay question has no obvious answer)
- Below grade 9 (the metacognitive and structural demands are too high)

## Schreibgespräch (Silent Written Conversation)

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Einstieg, Erarbeitung
**Time:** 10–20 min

**Procedure:**
1. A provocative question or stimulus is written at the top of a large sheet (or digital document).
2. Students write responses **in silence** — no talking allowed.
3. They read each other's contributions and respond in writing: agreeing, disagreeing, adding, questioning.
4. The "conversation" grows organically on the paper.
5. After 10–15 min, teacher selects interesting threads for verbal discussion.

**Variations:**
- Rotating Schreibgespräch: multiple sheets circulate; students respond to different conversations.
- Digital Schreibgespräch: shared document or chat-like platform.
- Bilingual Schreibgespräch (English classes): allow German for complex thoughts, encourage English for practice.

**Avoid when:**
- Students treat it as individual note-taking without responding to each other (emphasize the "conversation" aspect)
- A single student dominates the paper space
- No verbal follow-up happens (the written conversation needs synthesis)
- Used repeatedly without variation (novelty wears off)
