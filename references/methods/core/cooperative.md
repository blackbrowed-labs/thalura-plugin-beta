# Cooperative Learning Methods

Methods that structure group interaction so every student contributes. Versatile across both subjects and all grade levels.

---

## Think-Pair-Share

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Any (Einstieg, Erarbeitung, Sicherung)
**Time:** 8–15 min

**Procedure:**
1. **Think** (2–5 min): Students reflect individually on a question, text, or prompt. They jot down notes.
2. **Pair** (3–5 min): Each student discusses their ideas with a partner.
3. **Share** (5 min): Selected pairs report key insights to the class. Teacher synthesizes.

**Variations:**
- Think-Write-Pair-Share: adds a written component before pairing.
- Think-Pair-Square: pairs merge into fours before sharing with the class.

**Avoid when:**
- Task is purely mechanical (grammar drills, vocabulary matching)
- Immediate individual assessment is needed
- Overused without genuine thinking tasks (students stop taking it seriously)

## Placemat (Placemat-Methode)

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Sammlung
**Time:** 15–25 min

**Procedure:**
1. Groups of 4 sit around a large sheet divided into 4 outer sections + a center field.
2. Each student writes ideas **silently** in their own section (5–10 min).
3. Sheet is rotated so everyone reads the others' contributions.
4. Group discussion: compare, contrast, debate the ideas.
5. Group writes a joint result (consensus, synthesis, or structured comparison) in the center.
6. Optional: Gallery Walk of all placemats.

**Variations:**
- Digital Placemat: shared document with color-coded sections.
- Placemat with roles: each student approaches the question from a different angle (e.g. ethical, practical, emotional, historical).

**Avoid when:**
- Question is too narrow for multiple perspectives
- Groups are very unbalanced in ability or motivation
- Time < 15 min (rushed Placemats produce shallow results)

## Kugellager (Inside-Outside Circle / Ball Bearing)

**Grades:** 5–10 (can work in Oberstufe but feels less natural for older students)
**Phase:** Übung, Sicherung, Einstieg
**Time:** 10–20 min

**Procedure:**
1. Half the class forms an inner circle facing **outward**; the other half forms an outer circle facing **inward**. Each person faces a partner.
2. Teacher gives a prompt or question. Partners discuss for 2–3 min.
3. Signal: outer circle shifts one position clockwise. New partner.
4. New prompt (or same prompt for variety of perspectives). Discuss 2–3 min.
5. Repeat 4–6 rounds. Debrief key findings.

**Variations:**
- Expert Kugellager: inner circle are "experts" who explain a topic; outer circle asks questions.
- Speed-dating format: each pair has only 60 seconds.

**Avoid when:**
- Room is too small for two circles with movement
- Topics require deep reflection (this is a quick-exchange format)
- Odd number of students (form one trio)
- Noise is a concern for neighboring rooms

## Jigsaw (Gruppenpuzzle / Expertengruppen)

**Grades:** 7–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung
**Time:** 40–60 min (needs a full lesson)

**Procedure:**
1. Divide material into 4–5 segments (different aspects of a topic, different texts, different thinkers).
2. Assign each student one segment. Students with the same segment meet in **expert groups** (15–20 min) to study it thoroughly.
3. Students return to **home groups** (each home group has one expert per segment).
4. Each expert teaches their segment to the home group (5 min per expert).
5. Home group completes a synthesis task (answering questions that require knowledge from all segments).

**Variations:**
- Double Jigsaw: expert phase includes two rounds (read, then discuss) before teaching.
- Jigsaw with product: each home group creates a poster or summary combining all segments.

**Avoid when:**
- Material is too interconnected to divide meaningfully
- Students lack maturity for unsupervised expert-group work
- Time < 45 min (rushed Jigsaws lead to superficial expert knowledge)
- One segment is significantly harder than others (unbalanced expertise)

## Gallery Walk (Museumsgang / Galerierundgang)

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Sicherung, Präsentation
**Time:** 20–30 min

**Procedure:**
1. Groups create visual work products (posters, mind maps, analysis charts, infographics).
2. Products are displayed at "stations" around the room.
3. Students walk in small groups, examine each station. They leave written feedback (sticky notes, comment cards, or a feedback column on the poster).
4. Optional: one "host" stays at each station to explain and answer questions.
5. Plenary discussion synthesizes findings across all stations.

**Variations:**
- Silent Gallery Walk: no talking — all feedback is written.
- Critical Gallery Walk: each visitor must leave one question and one compliment.

**Avoid when:**
- All groups worked on identical tasks with identical results (nothing new to discover)
- No time for the walking phase (< 15 min active walking)
- Products lack substance (Gallery Walk just amplifies shallow work)

## Snowballing (Schneeball-Methode)

**Grades:** 6–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Sammlung
**Time:** 15–25 min

**Procedure:**
1. **Individual** (2–3 min): Each student writes down their ideas/answers.
2. **Pair** (3 min): Share with one partner, agree on best ideas.
3. **Foursome** (5 min): Two pairs merge, discuss, and refine to the strongest 2–3 points.
4. **Eights or Plenary** (5 min): Groups present their refined results to the class.

**Variations:**
- Reverse Snowball: start with class brainstorm, then small groups prioritize, then individuals select.
- Snowball into writing: the final group product becomes the outline for an individual essay.

**Avoid when:**
- Class size < 12 (too few levels of merging)
- Topic doesn't benefit from progressive refinement
- Later rounds just repeat earlier ideas without adding depth
