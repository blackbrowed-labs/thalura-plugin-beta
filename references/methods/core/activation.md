# Activation & Warm-Up Methods

Quick methods to open a lesson, activate prior knowledge, or energize the class. Low preparation, high impact. Best for Einstieg and transitions.

---

## Brainstorming / Clustering

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Einstieg
**Time:** 5–10 min

**Procedure:**
1. Write the topic/keyword on the board or in the center of a shared document.
2. Students call out (or write on sticky notes) all associations — **no criticism, no filtering**.
3. Collect until the flow stops (2–5 min).
4. **Clustering:** organize the ideas into categories together. Identify themes and connections.
5. Use the clustered results as a springboard for the lesson.

**Variations:**
- Silent brainstorming: students write on cards individually first, then pool.
- Reverse brainstorm: "What would make [topic] go wrong?" — then flip to positive approaches.
- Digital brainstorming: Mentimeter, Padlet, or AnswerGarden for live word clouds.

**Avoid when:**
- Used without a follow-up that connects to the lesson (brainstorming isn't the activity — it's the launch)
- Same dominant students always speak (use silent variant)
- Topic is too narrow for genuine associative thinking

## Buzz Groups / Murmelgruppe

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Einstieg, Zwischensicherung (micro-break during input phases)
**Time:** 2–5 min

**Procedure:**
1. After a teacher input, reading task, or question: "Turn to your neighbor and discuss for 2 minutes."
2. Students exchange reactions, questions, or summaries with their immediate neighbor.
3. Teacher brings class back. Selected pairs share key points.

**Purpose:** Breaks up long input phases. Lets every student process and articulate ideas before plenary discussion. Prevents the "3 students answer every question" problem.

**Variations:**
- Buzz + write: after the buzz, each student writes a one-sentence takeaway.
- Timed buzz: strict 60-second exchange to maintain energy.

**Avoid when:**
- Overused to the point of routine (students stop engaging seriously)
- No clear prompt is given (vague "discuss" leads to off-topic chat)
- Used as a substitute for genuine pair or group work on complex tasks

## Blitzlicht (Lightning Round)

**Grades:** 5–S4
**Phase:** Einstieg, Sicherung, Reflexion
**Time:** 5–10 min

**Procedure:**
1. Pose a focused question (e.g. "One word that describes your reaction to the text", "Name one argument for/against", "What's still unclear?").
2. Go around the room: each student answers in **one sentence** (max). No discussion, no replies.
3. Teacher notes recurring themes or surprising responses.
4. Brief synthesis: "I noticed many of you mentioned X — let's explore that."

**Variations:**
- Written Blitzlicht: students write on index cards instead of speaking (anonymous option).
- Emoji Blitzlicht: students hold up an emoji card or write an emoji to express their response.
- Exit ticket: Blitzlicht at the end of the lesson as a formative assessment.

**Avoid when:**
- Class is very large (>25) and the round takes too long (use written variant)
- Students just repeat what the person before them said (vary the question or start from different points)
- The question requires complex answers that can't fit in one sentence

## Gamification (Spielerische Elemente)

**Grades:** 5–10 (Oberstufe students may find it infantilizing unless designed maturely)
**Phase:** Einstieg, Übung, Sicherung
**Time:** 10–20 min

**Procedure/Examples:**
- **Vocabulary games:** Taboo, Pictionary, Bingo, Memory with English terms.
- **Quiz formats:** Kahoot, Jeopardy, Who Wants to Be a Millionaire adapted for content review.
- **Race formats:** teams compete to complete tasks correctly and quickly.
- **Role-based games:** Mystery scenarios where students must use English to solve a puzzle.
- **Philosophy adaptations:** "Would you rather?" with philosophical dilemmas, concept Taboo (describe a concept without using key terms).

**Avoid when:**
- Competition overshadows learning (some students shut down when losing)
- The game doesn't practice the actual skill (fun but educationally empty)
- Used too frequently (loses its motivational effect)
- Oberstufe classes unless explicitly framed as a review strategy
- Noise level is inappropriate for the setting
