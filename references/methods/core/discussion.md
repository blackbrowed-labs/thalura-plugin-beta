# Discussion & Dialogue Methods

Methods centered on structured conversation and argumentation — core to Philosophy, valuable for English communicative competence.

---

## Socratic Dialogue (Sokratisches Gespräch)

**Grades:** 7–S4 (simplified 7–8, full from 9)
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Vertiefung
**Time:** 20–45 min

**Procedure:**
1. Pose a guiding philosophical question (e.g. "What is justice?", "Can we know anything for certain?").
2. Students offer initial answers.
3. Through targeted follow-up questions, guide students to discover contradictions, hidden assumptions, and logical gaps in their own reasoning.
4. Students refine their positions iteratively. The goal is sharpened thinking, not a single "correct" answer.
5. Close with a reflection: What did we learn about our own assumptions?

**Variations:**
- Neo-Socratic method (Leonard Nelson): one student shares a concrete experience, the group abstracts a principle from it.
- Student-led: an advanced student takes the facilitator role.

**Avoid when:**
- Time < 20 min (needs space for genuine back-and-forth)
- Students have zero background on the topic (provide a short text first)
- Class > 25 without clear speaking structure (combine with Fishbowl)
- English classes below Oberstufe (language barrier prevents philosophical depth)

## Fishbowl Discussion (Fishbowl-Diskussion)

**Grades:** 7–S4 (introduce gradually from 7)
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Sicherung
**Time:** 20–35 min

**Procedure:**
1. Arrange 4–6 chairs in an inner circle, remaining chairs in an outer circle.
2. Inner circle discusses the topic/thesis. One chair stays **empty**.
3. Any observer can sit in the empty chair to contribute, then return to the outer circle.
4. Teacher moderates minimally (only if discussion stalls or rules are broken).
5. Outer circle takes structured notes (e.g. argument tracking sheet, rhetorical strategies observed).
6. Debrief: outer circle shares observations, class evaluates the quality of arguments.

**Variations:**
- Rotating Fishbowl: after 10 min, inner circle is replaced entirely.
- Panel Fishbowl: inner circle members represent assigned positions (pro/contra/moderate).

**Avoid when:**
- Students haven't learned basic discussion rules yet (practice with Buzz Groups first)
- Topic doesn't generate genuine controversy (discussion dies quickly)
- Some students dominate while others are too shy — use Think-Pair-Share beforehand to build confidence

## Pro-Contra Debate (Pro-Kontra-Debatte / Podiumsdiskussion)

**Grades:** 8–S4
**Phase:** Erarbeitung, Transfer
**Time:** 30–45 min (incl. preparation)

**Procedure:**
1. Present a debatable thesis (e.g. "AI should be regulated by government", "Utilitarianism is the best ethical framework").
2. Divide into Pro and Contra groups. Each group prepares arguments (15–20 min).
3. Representatives deliver opening statements (2 min each).
4. Rebuttals and free debate phase. A moderator (student or teacher) manages turns.
5. Audience evaluates arguments using a rubric (strength of evidence, rhetorical quality, responsiveness).
6. Debrief: which arguments were strongest and why? Did anyone change their mind?

**Variations:**
- British Parliamentary style (4 teams: opening/closing government and opposition)
- Fish-market debate: everyone stands, physically moves to pro/contra side, can switch mid-debate

**Avoid when:**
- Topics are deeply personal (students' own religious beliefs, family situations)
- No preparation time available (leads to superficial arguments)
- Class lacks a respectful discussion culture (establish ground rules first)

## Philosophical Conversation (Philosophisches Gespräch)

**Grades:** 5–S4 (adapted to age)
**Phase:** Einstieg, Erarbeitung
**Time:** 15–30 min

**Procedure:**
1. Present a stimulus: a short text, image, story, quote, or thought experiment.
2. Students formulate their own questions in response (individually, then shared).
3. The class selects one central question to explore together.
4. Open conversation: students share views, respond to each other, build on ideas. Teacher participates as a co-thinker, not authority.
5. Teacher ensures logical rigor, introduces relevant philosophical concepts when appropriate.
6. Closing round: each student shares one insight or remaining question.

**Variations:**
- Philosophy for Children (P4C) format: community of inquiry with explicit thinking moves.
- Stimulus rotation: different stimuli at different tables, groups rotate.

**Avoid when:**
- A specific factual outcome is needed (this method is open-ended by design)
- Severe time pressure (genuine reflection needs unhurried space)
- English classes where language proficiency doesn't allow for nuanced abstract conversation
